# Build the research story before the map

## The question, intervention and bounded contribution

Cross-read the abstract, the end of the introduction, results, discussion/conclusion and key figures. Establish three distinct points: the concrete bottleneck in previous work, what design or comparison the authors changed and why it could help, and what was actually demonstrated under the tested conditions. Keep hoped-for applications separate. Work backward from the contribution to the premises that must hold: sample identity, a discriminating control, a particular state or a signal assignment.

Do not reconstruct the whole paper from its abstract. Methods and figures are not equally important, and an author claim is not automatically an established result.

## Track objects and comparisons

Keep a small internal record of object, preparation/treatment, state during measurement, comparison partner and measured quantity. Distinguish precursor from final product, a molecular solution from a polymer network, a designed structure from a characterized specimen, and a dry micrograph from a material in operation. Distinguish whether a reaction occurs, whether the desired product forms, how much forms and how much is isolated. Preserve denominator, normalization, loading history, units and reference treatment when they affect the inference.

For each decisive judgment, identify the observation or comparison that supports it and the stronger claim it does not establish. Explain what a control shares and changes. Several varying conditions make a formulation comparison useful without making it a controlled single-variable experiment. Separate evidence not yet read from evidence genuinely absent in the supplied source.

For chemistry, consider product identity before assigning yield or selectivity; do not add unassigned chromatographic peaks to invent a recalculated ee. For catalytic or cyclic responses, explain the relevant regeneration or material/energy handoff when the paper supports it. Label proposed mechanisms as proposed. For materials, connect design variable → actual structure/state → property/function → qualified interpretation. Do not force every paper into the same route or number of stages.

## Transitions and branch depth

For adjacent stages, write internally: what is now known, what remains unknown, and why the next test addresses it. If the transition only says “then the authors measured the next figure,” the reasoning is not yet organized. Combine several characterizations that answer one question; a single composite may support multiple stages.

Establish a substantial sample transition early and match its depth across the paper. The main story contains the reason for the study, design rationale, decisive comparisons, actual observations and bounded conclusions. Essential SI belongs here when needed. Do not hide all facts in branches and leave the trunk with statements such as “performance improved.”

A branch should solve a specific comprehension problem: interpreting paired moduli, assigning an NMR reference, understanding a normalization, identifying a specimen or explaining a synthetic connection. Explain which trunk step it deepens and how the reader returns. It needs positive teaching and relevant evidence, not just “this does not prove…” warnings. Keep concepts and illustrative analogies close to the place where they help; label invented teaching examples.

## Figure learning tasks

Inspect the actual figure and surrounding text. Before interpreting a panel, identify specimen and state, axes/units/scales, legend and comparison groups. Lead the reader to a visible observation, then explain how it changes the current judgment. Choose an order by the research question rather than always following a–b–c.

Keep shared axes, full legends, scale bars and necessary paired panels together. Cropping must not remove the information needed to identify or compare data. Return to the complete original composite and source page. Small labels require magnification; unresolved ambiguity requires narrower wording, not guesses. A caption covering several panels does not imply every panel contains the same metric or condition.

SI may establish identity, add a negative control, supply a calibration or expose a condition behind a main-text comparison. Treat it as part of the explanation, not an appendix of miscellaneous pictures. Do not compare curve heights across arbitrary scaling, incompatible normalization, extinction coefficients, media or specimen states. Distinguish chemical change, macroscopic gelation, wet mechanics and dry morphology.

## Same content, two maps, one canvas

Name trunk stages and branches by scientific questions or judgments, with figure numbers identifying their purpose. The story map uses the same records as the prose: headings, numbers, side assignment and targets. Separately author the scientific argument graph; its edges can mean support, comparison, interpretation, a next question or a boundary. Give each edge a concrete meaning. Do not infer causality from reading order.

The trunk and branches coexist on the same paper. The default view fits the trunk for reading; it does not shrink everything to include large empty branch areas. Selecting a branch moves and zooms the camera with a continuous connection-aware animation. It must not reparent content, replace the trunk, navigate to a secondary page or simulate camera movement by toggling layout. Returning restores the original pose and focus. Check node coordinates separately from camera coordinates.

Preserve blank-space panning, selectable text, clickable source figures, zoom, wheel reading, keyboard controls and reduced motion. Side arrows belong on the side where their branches actually sit. Camera movement and scrolling update map location without stealing focus. A narrow-screen map can collapse while retaining the same canvas and readable prose at reading scale. Do not add accounts, cloud libraries, 3D graphs or chat merely to implement reading navigation.

## Separate acceptance questions

Read the text with the map hidden and retell the argument. Ask a few paper-specific questions whose answers must be recoverable from the companion and source. Check whether a complex-figure branch actually enables the intended reading task. Word count, branch count, keyword matches, model confidence or a passing ledger do not establish depth.

Then inspect each map target, camera action, source link, image quality and SI placement. A successful interface test does not certify scientific correctness; a complete analysis ledger does not demonstrate improved human comprehension.
