# Semantic audit of the story tree

Use this after drafting a full companion, before final source review/rendering. The output is still the reader; keep this audit internal or available for inspection, not a second website the reader must study. This is a human/agent meaning check, not an automatic fluency score.

## Reconstruct the source's contribution before judging the retelling

Map each stated research question and central result to an original anchor and then to its place in the trunk or a branch. Record a reason for any deliberately omitted major item and disclose that scope. Distinguish central findings from optional detail: a demographic interaction or a decisive subgroup result may need a short trunk signpost even when its full explanation belongs in a branch. Do not replace the paper's answer with only limitations.

Check how the conclusion answers the opening question. If a paper asks whether a result transfers across fields, explain the authors' actual comparison and interpretation while retaining its limits; a closing generic warning does not complete that story. Preserve the contrast with prior work as the source presents it, without claiming that cited prior work was independently read.

## Read the trunk with all branches closed

Can the intended first-time reader explain why the problem matters, what the authors do, why the approach could help, what they actually find and what that changes? Keep the concrete meaning of a method or predictor in the trunk where it carries the argument. A heading such as “subgroup results” needs relevant actual results, not merely names of variables and cautionary sentences.

Before each transition, ask what is now known, what remains open and why the next step follows. Define the first load-bearing use of a symbol, unit, score, category or conceptual distinction. Keep original and normalized objects distinct, explain how continuous scores become categories, and do not mix classification levels. For a proof, unpack the particular deduction that joins the premises; naming a lemma is not explaining the decisive step.

## Make branches deepen this paper

Each branch solves a specific comprehension problem and reconnects to the current claim. Remove branches that only explain how the reader software works, why a template element is absent, or generic AI cautions. A useful branch may show how a figure defines a predictor, why two statistical tests ask different questions, what a proof assumption does, or how an objection changes a conditional argument. Necessary source/version notes remain accessible without displacing the paper's positive reasoning.

The story map must use the same section/branch records as the prose. Recheck the separate argument graph after semantic changes: its findings must match the reviewed canonical claims, its destinations must explain those claims, and its edge types must preserve support, comparison, assumption and interpretation. Reading order is not a substitute for those relations.

## Compare meanings, then revise

For each important change, retain the source anchor, old reader location, diagnosis and new explanation. Check that numbers, signs, scope and speaker attribution survived; also check omissions and missing bridges. A paragraph may contain no false sentence yet fail to teach the original argument because its central finding is missing. Finish by repeating the source comparison and trunk-only read. Label same-agent review honestly; good hashes, a reviewer-style format and working navigation do not establish independent evaluation or measured reader comprehension.
